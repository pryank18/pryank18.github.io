# Pravesh — Market Requirements Document

## Market problem

Competitive-exam preparation in India is long, solitary and high-stakes. Aspirants spend one to three years preparing, often away from home or in coaching hubs, and the most common failure is not missing material but losing consistency. Families who fund the preparation have no reliable view of effort, so they rely on asking, which increases pressure without improving information.

## Target market

- **Primary:** aspirants aged 18–25 preparing for UPSC, SSC, Banking, State PSC, NEET, JEE and CAT, including droppers and school students in classes 11–12
- **Secondary:** their parents and mentors
- **Language:** Hindi and Hinglish first, English alongside; regional languages added only when demand and a native reviewer exist
- **Device:** mobile browser first, installable as a web app

## Why now

- Smartphone access is near-universal among aspirants, including in smaller cities
- The leading prep platforms have consolidated around content and test series, leaving the habit and family layer unserved
- India's DPDP Act 2023 raises the bar for handling minors' data; a product designed for consent from day one is easier for families to trust than one retrofitted later
- AI makes a multilingual help layer cheap to run, as long as safety text stays hand-written

## Competitive landscape

- **Content and test platforms (Testbook, Adda247, Unacademy, PhysicsWallah):** strong on material and mocks, built for the aspirant alone, monetised through subscriptions
- **Generic habit and study trackers:** track time but know nothing about exam syllabi, mock trends or family support
- **Coaching institute apps:** tied to one institute's batch, end when the course ends
- **Status quo:** notebooks, WhatsApp check-ins with parents, and anxiety; free, but gives no honest picture

## Value proposition

For the aspirant: a two-minute daily loop that shows whether they are actually on pace for their exam, in their own language, for free.

For the family: a calm weekly summary that leads with wins and shows only what the aspirant chooses to share, so support replaces interrogation.

## Positioning

The accountability layer that sits beside any prep platform, not a competitor to them. Pravesh does not compete on content; it makes whatever content an aspirant already uses more likely to get done.

## Differentiators

- Family-first, with the aspirant in control of sharing
- Built for minors' consent from the first screen
- No paywall, ads, contact harvesting or location tracking
- One language at a time, never a half-translated screen

## Status

Live and ready for a soft launch with 15–20 known users. Pricing stays voluntary; institute licensing is a later option once retention is proven.
