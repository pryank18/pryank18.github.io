03 / CASES

Behind the decisions.

Written the way I run product reviews: problem first, evidence second, scope last. Commercial terms stay private.

Shipped

Vendor onboarding in under half a day

A B2B vendor platform for petrol-pump owners at a fuel-delivery startup in India. 150+ vendors live.

Problem

Bringing a pump on as a vendor took a full working day of document collection and checks, then a second review the next day, so no new vendor could take orders the day they signed up.

Field evidence

300+ conversations with petrol-pump owners, at their pumps rather than over a survey. They showed which checks repeated for every vendor and could be standardised.

What I built

Standard validation and compliance rules inside the onboarding flow, so a submission was checked once, against one rulebook, at the moment of entry instead of the next morning.

Result

Onboarding came down to under half a day, for 150+ vendors now live on the platform.

The lesson

The fix was a rule set more than a feature, and it came from the pumps, not from a document.

Shipped

A new country, from zero to 1.5M litres a month

Choosing a market, then launching B2B bulk fuel in Zimbabwe and running the business it became.

Problem

The company wanted to expand into Africa, with no local presence, suppliers, banking or customers in any of the candidate countries.

Evidence

Evaluated Zimbabwe, Zambia and Nigeria, then picked Zimbabwe. On the ground, 40+ discovery interviews with B2B fuel buyers shaped what to sell first and what the product would need later.

What I built

The business before the product: banking, procurement, credit, dispatch and reporting under written SOPs, with ~15 suppliers live in month one. The interviews became FMS and VMS requirements; an on-site FMS for a major beverage bottler was scoped, then paused pending hardware sourcing.

Result

~1.5M litres a month by month ten, 50+ repeat B2B customers in nine months, and a promotion to Country Head while keeping the product role.

The lesson

In a new market, the first product is the operating model. Software earns its place once the volume is real.

Operating principles

  • Go to the site before writing the spec.The best fuel products I've shipped started with a walk around a depot, not a survey form.
  • Price on value you can prove.If the buyer can verify the saving, they'll share it.
  • A metric without a baseline is a wish.Every goal gets a starting number and a date.
  • Say what we won't build.A scope is only as good as its non-goals list.
  • Teams own the how, leadership owns the why.My job is strategy, trade-offs and clearing the path.
  • AI in the workflow, not on the slide.Agents compress discovery, prototyping and ops; measure the time saved like any feature.

My first 90 days in a new product role

  1. Days 1–30

    Listen

    Customer and site visits, data audit, P&L by product line, 1:1s with every product, engineering and sales lead. Output: a written diagnosis.

  2. Days 31–60

    Focus

    Pick the one or two bets that matter, write the kill and park list, set baselines. Output: a one-page strategy and a scorecard the CEO and team sign.

  3. Days 61–90

    Ship and build the system

    First visible win against a baseline, an operating cadence, and a hiring plan for the gaps.
